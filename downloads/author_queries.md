# Experimental information needed to complete the sand paper

These questions identify the remaining experimental gaps. Existing notebooks, calibration files or processing scripts may answer many of them; new measurements should be chosen only after those records are checked.

## First priority

1. **What are ST1–ST5?** Origin or supplier, batch, collection/preparation history and intended application. Are the five samples different compositions, size fractions, treatments or sources?
2. **Where are the original measurements?** Native instrument files, sample/reference single-beam spectra, timestamps and reduction/plotting scripts. Export spectra without smoothing or clipping as well as the final processed curves.
3. **What are the shaded bands?** Specify the statistic, number of observations, units, absolute/relative convention and coverage factor. Is the central curve a mean, fitted curve or single spectrum?
4. **What is Figure 1(d)?** Does its blue curve contain ST4 data with a copied ST1 label, or was the wrong dataset plotted? Verify from filenames and plotting code before editing the legend.
5. **How was Infragold used?** Separate standard or sphere-wall region; certificate/serial identifier; calibrated reflectance spectrum and uncertainty; why 0.95 was taken constant; date, condition and supported wavelength range.
6. **What does “100 scans” mean?** Individual spectra, interferograms coadded into one spectrum, or 100 independently repeated measurements? How was the stated standard deviation calculated?
7. **Was the sand bed opaque?** Bed thickness, holder material, backing and any thickness/backing/transmission measurements. Otherwise report the effective bed–holder assembly, with its conditions.
8. **What exactly was the sphere configuration?** Specular inclusion/exclusion, port closure, reference geometry, sample-to-port contact, beam footprint, angular spread and any substitution/throughput correction.

## Method and comparison

9. Record actual temperature and room conditions; spectrometer and sphere model; source, beamsplitter, detector/window; resolution, sampling, apodization, phase correction, gain and acquisition sequence.
10. Record purge/vacuum conditions, elapsed time between reference and sample, atmospheric correction and any detector/source changes or stitched spectral ranges.
11. Describe “smoothed” and “rough”: tool, preparation procedure, bed depth/mass, compaction, surface slope/height characterization, and photographs. Prefer “levelled” and “irregular” if that is what was done.
12. Give independent filling counts, repositioning repeats and spatial sampling. Identify shared references so that covariance in paired differences can be retained.
13. Explain the source of the 2% alignment estimate and what quantity it bounds. Is it a standard uncertainty, expanded uncertainty, relative bound or absolute reflectance bound?
14. Supply grain-size distributions, phase composition and moisture history if available. Do not assume a dried state or a quartz-rich composition from the word “sand.”

## If additional work is needed

The minimum discriminating additions are a bed-thickness/opacity check; independent refilling repeats; a calibrated reference/transfer check over the accepted spectral interval; and particle-size, phase and moisture characterization. Choose repeat counts from the desired precision and observed variability, not from an arbitrary claim that three repetitions suffice.

An angular series is necessary only if the intended claim extends to exact normal or hemispherical emissivity or angular behavior. Elevated-temperature measurements are necessary if the application requires material properties at elevated temperature. Neither should be implied by the current room-temperature, fixed-angle dataset.

To test preparation equivalence, specify the scientifically acceptable difference before evaluating it, and decide whether it applies wavelength by wavelength or to a predetermined band metric. A nonsignificant difference alone does not establish equivalence.
